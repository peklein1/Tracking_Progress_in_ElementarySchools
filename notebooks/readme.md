Project Overview
This project analyzes and tracks how students progress through elementary school, from pre-kindergarten through fifth grade, across both academic performance and behavioral outcomes. Because the available public datasets report data at the school and grade level rather than for individual students, progress is tracked at the grade-cohort level within a school as a practical proxy for individual student growth. By combining school-level academic proficiency data with discipline and attendance records, the project aims to uncover whether patterns in behavior (such as chronic absenteeism or disciplinary incidents) correlate with academic trends across grade levels. Understanding these relationships matters because it can help teachers and administrators identify at-risk grade transitions early and target support before performance gaps widen.

Key Analytical Questions
Is there a relationship between behavioral indicators (discipline incident rate, chronic absenteeism) and academic proficiency (reading/math) at the school level, across grades K–5?
Do academic proficiency and behavioral trends shift at particular grade-level transitions (e.g., K–2 vs. 3–5)?

Secondary analytical question
How do proficiency and discipline trends change year-over-year within the same schools?
Do schools with higher chronic absenteeism show slower proficiency growth over time?
Are there notable differences between grade bands in the severity or frequency of discipline incidents?
